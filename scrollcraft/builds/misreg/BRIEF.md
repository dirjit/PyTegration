# MISREG · Misregister Press

> **Self-authored, not interviewed.**
> The human chose four things directly: self-author the brief, distinct scenes
> (not one unbroken world), maximalist, and no raster assets. Everything below
> is written in the brand's voice as a fallback, per SKILL.md Step 0. It is not
> a record of what a client said.

A risograph print studio. Two drums, flat spot colour, halftone, and
misregistration kept rather than corrected.

---

## The eight answers

**1. Vibe in three to five words, plus references.**

> "Loud, off-register, ink-heavy, cheap on purpose."
>
> References: a xeroxed gig flyer stapled to a lamppost and rained on. The Goo
> cover. A konbini receipt roll, still warm.

**2. The scroll journey, section by section, in their words.**

> "First you hit the ink. Not a logo, the ink. Then you find out we only own two
> drums and that that's the entire point. Then the colours start stacking, pink
> pulled over blue makes a purple you cannot get any other way. Then the
> mistakes: the slip, the roller marks, the ghosting, we keep all of it. Then
> the press locks up and prints one clean thing, and that is the moment. Then
> how to send us a job. Then out, no goodbye."

**3. The energy curve.**

> "Loud from the first frame and it stays loud. One place near the end where it
> goes completely still, and that stillness should feel louder than the loud
> parts. Then abrupt out. No wind-down, no thank-you-for-scrolling."

**4. How should someone feel, stage by stage, and the ONE moment.**

> "Jolt, then recognition, then greed for the colour, then affection for the
> mess, then awe when it locks, then decision.
>
> The one moment: the two inks slam into register and the poster is suddenly,
> briefly, perfect. Then they drift apart again, because that is what actually
> happens on a real press."

**5. One thing this site should do that no site they have seen does.**

> "Show the misregistration as the interface. Every website in the world fixes
> its alignment. Ours should be visibly out of register the whole way down, and
> snap true exactly once."

**6. How far from premium-minimal.**

> "As far as it goes. Maximalist. Fluorescent, layered, too much. Premium
> minimal is the thing our clients are running away from."

**7. One unbroken world, or distinct scenes?**

> "Distinct scenes. Cuts. A riso print is one pull of a drum, then another pull
> on top of it. It is not a camera move. It is a stack of separate impressions,
> and the page should be built the same way."

**8. What assets do they already have?**

> "Nothing photographic, and we do not want any. The work is flat spot colour
> and halftone. Photograph it and you lose it: you get a picture of a print
> instead of a print. Build it out of the actual ink."

## The feeling curve

The unit of feeling in a rhythmic cutlist is the **run of cuts**, not the
individual cut, so the curve is written per movement. Fourteen acts group into
seven movements. The adjacency rule from feel.md §1 (no two neighbours carrying
the same feeling) is enforced at movement level.

```
A  (acts 1-2)    Jolt          full-bleed fluoro pink, the word cuts in before you have settled
B  (acts 3-4)    Recognition   two drums, named plainly on paper ground, the constraint stated
C  (acts 5-7)    Greed         the ink stack builds, each cut lays one more colour down
D  (acts 8-10)   Affection     the faults, enlarged and labelled, kept on purpose
E  (act 11)      Silence       authored empty. Ink black, nothing on it, no cue
F  (act 12)      Awe           THE PEAK. the plates slam into register and hold
G  (acts 13-14)  Decision      the job form, then one abrupt full-bleed cut to the ask
```

## The peak

> "The two inks slid apart the whole way down and then they slammed together
> and the poster was suddenly perfect, and then it went wrong again."

Lives in **movement F, act 12**, and in the fixed chrome that runs across acts
11 and 12.

The rhythmic cutlist grammar bans `pin` and `dwell` outright, which is exactly
the collision uniqueness.md §2.8 describes: the peak wants a hold and the
grammar forbids the device that holds. Resolved the way that section prescribes,
by moving the peak **out of the act stack and into the fixed chrome**. The two
ink plates in the top bar are a persistent element belonging to no act, so they
can converge, lock, hold as long as the peak needs, and break apart again while
the acts underneath keep cutting at full speed. No act on this page exceeds
1.4vh and nothing is pinned.

## Authored silence

**Act 11 is deliberately empty.** Ink-black ground, no copy, no cue, roughly
0.85 viewport-heights. It is the silence in front of the peak, per feel.md §2.
The verification pass will see a screen with nothing on it and should read it as
intent, not as dead scroll or a failed load.

## The tell-someone sentence

> It's the site where the logo is printed slightly wrong the whole way down, and
> then for about one second it prints perfectly.

## Honesty notes

- **No counters anywhere.** MISREG is a fictional studio invented for this
  sample, so every figure it could show would be invented. devices.md §7 is
  explicit that a fictional or pre-launch brand has no verified figures and
  therefore has no counters. The page carries no statistics.
- **Numbers that do appear are specification, not credibility.** Paper weight,
  ink names, drum count, screen ruling. These are facts about a described
  object, the way a museum label is, and none of them is a persuasion claim.
- The page states on its face that it is a sample build.
